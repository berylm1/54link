NERSA registered 144 private power plants totalling 3,129 MW between July and September — 139 solar plants (2,045 MW) and three wind farms (1,063 MW) — with investment of about R78.2 billion ($4.7 billion). That is nearly four times the 804 MW registered the previous quarter and lifts the total since 2018 to 23.3 GW.

World Bank: developing countries' external debt hits record $9.4 trillion

External debt of developing countries jumped 5.5% to a record $9.4 trillion in 2025 — the fastest pace since 2021 — according to the World Bank's International Debt Report 2026. Bond issuance by public entities rose nearly 35% to $251 billion, the highest in five years, while debt grew fastest (8.1%, to $1.3 trillion) in IDA-eligible countries, where new private-creditor borrowing now costs 8.2% on average, the highest since 1989.

Zambia pushes Tanzania power interconnector back to 2029

Zambia's electricity minister Peter Kapala told parliament the 613 km high-voltage line to Tanzania will now be commissioned in the second quarter of 2029, a year later than planned. Funded by $292 million in grants from the World Bank, EU and UK, the link is part of the 2,300 km Zambia-Tanzania-Kenya interconnector joining the Southern and Eastern Africa power pools, and the delay leaves Zambia without up to 500 MW of trading capacity until 2029.

African Union launches Continental Energy Security Policy Framework

Launched at the 36th African Hydro Symposium in Addis Ababa, the framework sets a common continental strategy covering electricity, bioenergy and oil and gas, aiming to strengthen regional power pools, expand cross-border electricity trade and mobilise investment in climate-resilient infrastructure. UNECA executive secretary Claver Gatete said energy security is increasingly a regional undertaking and called for coordinated planning among governments, utilities and regulators.

Ethiopia Rebased GDP Up Nearly 60% to $201 Billion

Ethiopia has revised the size of its economy up by 59.2% to $201.42 billion for 2024/25 after shifting its national accounts base year to 2024/25 from 2015/16, and puts 2025/26 GDP at $210.7 billion. The rebasing lowers debt-to-GDP and tax-to-GDP ratios as the country nears the end of its external debt restructuring, and is expected to feed into better credit ratings and lower borrowing costs. Kenya, Nigeria and South Africa have recently undertaken similar rebasing.
