Research: AfCFTA lifted African firms international-sales intensity by about 2.7 percentage points by 2022

Economists affiliated with the University of York, Queen Mary University of London, the IMF and Miami University, analysing 18,094 firm-year observations across 1,244 firms in 27 African economies, find that the ratio of international sales to total sales rose about 1 percentage point after the 2018 AfCFTA signing, reaching roughly 2.7 percentage points by 2022. Gains were concentrated among firms with prior export experience, which gained about 3.4 percentage points more than firms without it, and among firms able to finance production expansion. The authors argue market access alone will not deliver broad-based gains without trade finance, infrastructure, digital customs systems and export-readiness support.
